Swansea and flooding go back a long way

The town's own hazard mitigation report records the Great Hurricane of 1938 and Hurricane Carol in 1954. In that second storm, houses behind the sea wall and along Cove Street floated. In March 2010, days of rain flooded the town, closed schools and cut off emergency access, and the Wood Street bridge stayed closed for two years.

Residents lost furnaces and water heaters, and the report lists the southern end of town, the Grove, Touisset, Bushee Road and Cove Street among the flood-prone areas.

That history explains why flood response here is not an abstract idea. Homes close to Mount Hope Bay and the Lee, Palmer and Segreganset river corridors can see water from a tidal surge, a stalled storm or a blocked culvert. The town report notes that some culverts date back to the WPA era, which is one reason road flooding and yard flooding can show up quickly.

When flood water is already inside

Flood water carries dirt and bacteria, and it keeps soaking into anything porous. Do not wait for the water to recede on its own. Call at any hour, stay out of areas with live electrical equipment and tell us how deep the water is and where it entered.

Flood water is not clean water

Water that enters from outside has crossed yards, roads and, in some neighborhoods, septic systems. The town report describes roughly 500 homes in the Smoke Rise area with septic problems, so contaminated flood water is a genuine concern there. Treat it as unsafe to touch. Wear boots and gloves if you must walk through it, and keep children and pets away.

How a flood cleanup proceeds

1

Safety first

Check for submerged power, gas smells and structural damage. Stay out if the water reaches outlets or a panel.

2

Remove the water

Pumps and extraction units take out standing water. Mud and debris are removed with the water.

3

Remove what cannot be saved

Soaked carpet padding, wet insulation and porous materials touched by flood water usually have to go.

4

Clean and sanitize

Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products suited to the surface.

5

Dry and verify

Equipment dries the structure, and readings confirm it before any rebuild starts.

Flood insurance is a separate product

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states plainly that standard homeowners policies exclude flood, and that basement seepage is excluded as well. Flood coverage usually comes through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood policy. If you are not sure what you have, call your agent. We document the loss with photos and readings, and we do not decide coverage.

What to save and what to toss

  • Photograph every item before it leaves the house
  • Keep a list of discarded belongings with approximate values
  • Set aside irreplaceable photos and papers in a cool place for later drying
  • Throw away food, medicine and cosmetics that touched flood water
  • Do not run the furnace or HVAC system until it has been checked

After the water is out

Flood cleanup usually continues into structural drying and, depending on what stayed wet, mold remediation. Basements are the most common place for these losses, and our basement water damage page explains what that process looks like. After a storm, storm water damage cleanup covers roofs, windows and siding.

Shoreline homes in Ocean Grove sit in a part of town the hazard report lists at flood risk, and the page for that neighborhood explains what to do when river or bay water gets in.
