First, stop the water

Find the main shutoff valve and close it. In many Swansea houses it sits near the water meter in the basement or utility room. If you have no idea where it is, now is the time to learn, because a burst pipe can discharge gallons every minute. Once the supply is off, open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain what remains in the lines.

A plumber repairs the pipe. We do not replace pipes, and we will say so on the phone. What we do is remove the water the pipe released and dry the building so the damage does not keep growing. Swansea requires a permit for any installation or repair of plumbing, so ask your plumber about that before the repair is scheduled.

A burst pipe keeps running until it is shut off

Once the main valve is closed, the clock shifts to the wet materials. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring are easier to save the sooner they are dealt with. Our line is open at all hours, so a pipe that lets go on a Saturday night does not have to wait for Monday.

Where burst pipe water goes

Water follows gravity and framing. A split in a second-floor supply line runs down inside the wall cavity, soaks the insulation and drywall, then drips through the ceiling below. By the time a stain appears, the cavity may be saturated from top to bottom. That is why we use moisture meters and thermal imaging instead of judging by what is visible.

Inside walls

Wet insulation holds water against drywall and framing for days. It usually has to come out so the cavity can dry.

Under floors

Water travels along joists and collects in low spots, then soaks subfloor and the underside of flooring.

Through ceilings

Drywall ceilings sag when wet and can fail. We check them before anyone stands underneath.

Behind cabinets

Kitchen and bath supply lines often burst inside cabinets where the water stays hidden for hours.

Why older Swansea homes see these failures

Much of the housing around the south coast mixes eras. A newer addition may be plumbed with plastic or PEX while the original house still carries older copper or even galvanized steel. Galvanized lines corrode from the inside and can fail at threaded joints. Fittings where old and new materials meet are another common weak point.

The Swansea Water District treats its water for corrosion control, which is routine, but it cannot change the age of the pipe in your wall.

What the cleanup includes

  • Water removal from floors, carpet and low spots
  • Moisture mapping of walls, ceilings and subfloor
  • Removal of wet insulation and saturated drywall where drying will not work
  • Air movers and dehumidifiers placed and monitored
  • Photos and readings you can hand to your insurance carrier

Some of this overlaps with our ceiling water damage repair and wall and drywall repair work, which you can read about if the damage spread into those areas.

Burst pipes and your policy

Sudden and accidental discharge from plumbing is the kind of loss many homeowners policies are written to cover, but policies differ. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises reporting a claim promptly and reading the policy carefully. Take photos of the burst section if the plumber will let you keep it, and keep receipts for emergency work. We keep a record of the readings and the work we do.

If you are away when it happens

Seasonal and second homes along the Mount Hope Bay shoreline sit empty for long stretches. A pipe that fails in an empty house can run for days. If a neighbor, property manager or alarm company tells you there is water inside, call us from wherever you are. We will explain what we can do and what we need from you to get inside safely.

The standard behind the drying plan

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published industry reference for water damage restoration. Its core ideas, which are classifying the water, measuring moisture and setting a dry goal, are the structure behind the steps on this page.

What to do and what not to do first

  • Close the main valve and open a low faucet to drain the lines
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water
  • Do not walk under a sagging ceiling
  • Do not turn on light fixtures that have water in them
  • Photograph the split pipe and every wet area before cleanup
