Frozen pipes break open when they thaw

Ice inside a pipe does not usually cause the flood. It blocks the line and builds pressure, and the pipe splits. Water only starts to flow when the ice melts, which means the leak often begins hours after the coldest part of the night. People find it at breakfast, or when they come home from work and the ceiling is already wet.

If you find a frozen pipe that has not yet split, keep the faucet open a trickle, warm the area gently and call a plumber. If water is already flowing, close the main valve first and call us. Do not use an open flame to thaw a pipe.

The leak often starts after the thaw

A frozen pipe can hold until the temperature rises, then release water inside a wall or ceiling. Cold snaps tend to hit at night and over weekends, which is why our line stays open then. Call as soon as you see drips, stains or a drop in water pressure.

Why Swansea pipes freeze

Nor'easters bring wind off Mount Hope Bay that drives cold straight through exterior walls. Pipes in unheated crawl spaces, garages, kitchen sink cabinets on outside walls and uninsulated basements are the usual victims. Many shoreline cottages were built for summer and later converted for year round living, and some of their supply lines still run through spaces that were never meant to stay warm.

Outside walls

Sink and tub supply lines on a north-facing wall sit just behind cold siding.

Crawl spaces

Vented crawl spaces let the wind cool lines under the floor. See crawl space water damage.

Garages and porches

Washer hookups and hose bibs in unheated space freeze first.

Vacant homes

Heat set too low in a seasonal house gives pipes no protection during a deep freeze.

What to do when a pipe splits

  • Close the main water valve immediately
  • Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines
  • Turn off power to the wet area from a dry spot
  • Photograph the split pipe and the wet areas
  • Call a plumber for the repair and call us for the water

What we do after the plumber

Once the pipe is fixed, the building still holds water. We extract what is on the floor, open wet cavities where needed and set drying equipment. Frozen pipe losses often involve a ceiling below an upstairs bathroom, or a kitchen floor over a cold basement. Both can hide moisture for days after the visible water is gone. The same moisture mapping we use on a burst pipe loss applies here.

Cold weather drying is different

In winter the air is dry outdoors but cold, and opening windows is rarely a good drying plan. Heat and controlled dehumidification pull moisture out faster than ventilation does. We set equipment inside the building and keep the space warm enough to dry materials. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health notes that mold needs moisture to grow, so the goal is to bring materials back to normal readings quickly.

Protecting the house before the next freeze

  • Keep the thermostat at a steady setting, even when you travel
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during cold nights
  • Insulate exposed supply lines in crawl spaces and garages
  • Know where your main valve is and make sure it turns
  • Ask a neighbor to check a seasonal home during deep freezes

When the house is empty

Vacant and seasonal homes freeze more often than occupied ones, and the loss runs longer before anyone notices. If you are away, ask a neighbor to look in after a hard freeze, and consider a water leak sensor or a monitored thermostat. If someone reports water inside, call us and your plumber from wherever you are. We will explain what we need to enter safely.

Where the standard fits

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water damage restoration. It calls for measured drying with documented readings, which matters after a freeze because walls and floors can look dry while the cavity is not.
