What to Keep, Clean or Toss After Water Damage, Swansea MA

Three things decide whether an item is worth saving: what kind of water touched it, how long it stayed wet and what it is made of. Use this guide to sort belongings quickly.

Start with the water, not the item

Before you sort anything, find out what kind of water it was. Clean water from a broken supply line is a very different situation from flood water or a sewer backup. Clean water often allows salvage of many belongings if dried quickly. Water from a washing machine or dishwasher drain is gray, with soap and bacteria. Flood and sewage water are contaminated, and most porous items that touched them should be discarded.

Then look at the material

Item typeClean waterContaminated water
Solid wood furnitureOften can be dried and keptClean and dry if the finish allows, otherwise discard
Upholstered furniture and mattressesDry quickly or replaceDiscard
Carpet and paddingPadding often replaced, carpet may be driedDiscard
Clothing and linensWash and dry promptlyWash hot with disinfectant or discard
Books and papersFreeze and dry, see our note belowDiscard unless irreplaceable
Food and medicineDiscard if the container touched waterDiscard
Hard plastics and metalClean and dryClean and disinfect

The right choice depends on what the item is, how long it was wet and what the water carried. When in doubt, ask the crew on site.

The time factor

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health notes that absorbent materials not dried within 24 to 48 hours often need to be replaced. Items that were wet for several days, even with clean water, move toward the discard pile. Upholstery and mattresses in particular hold water and grow odors fast.

How to sort quickly

1

Move to a dry space

Get items off the wet floor and into a dry room or outdoors if the weather allows.

2

Photograph before you decide

Take a photo of anything you plan to discard.

3

Make three piles

Keep and dry, clean and test, and discard.

4

Keep a list

Write down what you discard, with approximate ages and values.

Keep a record for your insurer

Your carrier may ask for a list of damaged items. Photographs and a written list make the claim easier to process. The adjuster may want to see certain large items before you remove them, so ask before you carry a couch to the curb. The crew documents what is removed from the structure itself, such as carpet and drywall, and you document your own belongings.

Salvage you should not attempt yourself

Electronics, appliances and anything with a motor should be unplugged and checked by a technician. The rule is simple. Do not power up a wet device. For floors and cabinets that held water, see our kitchen water damage page and basement water damage page.

Cleaning what you decide to keep

For items that passed the sort, clean them before they dry with the dirt on. Rinse mud off hard items with clean water, then wash with detergent and let them dry completely. Disinfect hard surfaces that touched gray or flood water, using a product made for the job and following its directions. Never mix cleaning products, and open windows when you use them.

Fabric items go in the wash on the hottest setting the label allows. Anything that cannot be cleaned thoroughly and dried within a day or two moves to the discard pile. Keep your gloves on, wash your hands often and keep children out of the area until the cleanup is finished.

Large items such as sofas and dressers are the hardest calls. If you are unsure, photograph them and ask the crew on site, who can see the water line and the material. A short conversation before the curb pickup is better than regret afterward.

Why Swansea's flood history matters here

Swansea's hazard mitigation report notes that in March 2010 floods closed schools and cut off emergency access, and residents lost furnaces and water heaters. In the 1954 hurricane, houses along Cove Street floated. When a loss comes from outside water, assume contamination and be conservative about what you keep.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises homeowners to read their policy and report claims promptly. Ask your carrier what it needs for personal property claims before you discard large items.

For losses in Somerset or Rehoboth, the same approach holds, and our flood damage cleanup page covers the structure side.

Why water category decides the pile

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference that classifies water as clean, gray or contaminated. That classification is the basis for the keep, clean and discard decisions in this guide.

Local questions, answered

A mattress that was soaked is usually discarded. A small amount of clean water on the surface may be dried quickly, but it is hard to be sure the inside is dry.

Wash in hot water with detergent or disinfectant. Discard items that cannot be washed or that smell of sewage.

Clean-water books can sometimes be dried carefully. Irreplaceable ones are worth freezing until you can get help.

Discard food and medicine that touched flood water, and food in containers that cannot be sealed.

Solid wood often can be dried and kept if the water was clean and caught quickly. Particleboard swells and usually cannot.
