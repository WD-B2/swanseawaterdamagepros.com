Serving Rehoboth from Swansea

Rehoboth is about 7.7 miles from our Swansea office, and it is one of the largest towns in the region by area. Most of its homes are spread along rural roads, many with private wells and septic systems. The town hall sits on a former Nike missile site from 1956, a reminder of how much open land is here. That land is part of the town's character, and also part of why water problems can go unseen for a while.

When water is spreading in Rehoboth

Check the power

If an outage has stopped your sump pump, water can build fast. Keep clear of wet outlets and the panel until the power is safe.

Close the valves

Shut the main valve and, if you have a well, the pump breaker. This stops a broken line from refilling the house.

Move what you can

Lift furniture, boxes and electronics off wet floors, and photograph the damage before you start moving things.

Call with the address

Rehoboth roads are long and wooded, so give your street number and the nearest cross street when you call.

Dispatch for Rehoboth runs around the clock, which matters when a storm knocks out power and the sump pump with it.

March 2018 and what storms do here

In March 2018, a storm cut power to roughly 85 percent of Rehoboth and closed 32 roads, and the fire department pumped out houses. That is the town's own history, and it is the best example of how storm water, outages and failed sump pumps combine. Without power, pumps stop and basements fill. Our storm water damage cleanup page explains the process, and the basement water damage page covers what follows.

Wells and private water systems

Around 4,100 Rehoboth homes use private wells, according to town planning information. Well systems add a pump, a pressure tank and buried or exposed lines. A split line in a basement or a failed pressure tank can run a long time. The same pipe problems found anywhere in the area apply here as well, and our burst pipe water damage and frozen pipe pages explain them.

Culverts, bridges and flooding

The Danforth Street culvert on the West Branch of the Palmer River, the County Street bridge and the Route 44 bridge show up in the town's hazard planning. When they overtop, nearby yards and basements can flood. Rehoboth State Forest and the Redway Plain are large natural areas that shape drainage, and low spots near them can hold water for days after rain. See flood damage cleanup for what we do when water enters the house.

Large homes, long distances, remote lots

Rehoboth lots are big and homes are often set back from the road. Tell us how to find the house, whether a gravel driveway or gate is involved and whether the road is passable after a storm.
