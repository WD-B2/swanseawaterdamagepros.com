Black Mold Removal in Swansea, MA: What Owners Should Know

Black mold is a color, not a diagnosis. What matters is the moisture behind it and how the work is done. Here is what Swansea homeowners should understand before they scrub or hire.

What people mean by black mold

When homeowners say black mold, they usually mean any dark growth on a wall, ceiling, baseboard or basement surface. Color alone does not tell you what species it is or how serious it is, and a lab is not needed to know that something is wet and growing. What matters is where the moisture is coming from, how much material is affected and whether the growth is on hard surfaces or inside porous materials.

Moisture first, always

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health states that moisture is the cause of mold. Growth can begin when relative humidity stays above about 70 percent for a few days, and absorbent materials such as drywall, carpet padding and ceiling tile that are not dried within 24 to 48 hours often need to be replaced. Removing mold without fixing the water problem means it comes back.

Where it shows up in Swansea homes

Basements and crawl spaces

Near the bay and river corridors, ground water keeps lower levels damp. See crawl space water damage.

Bathrooms

Poor ventilation and a slow leak behind a tub or toilet can keep walls damp for months.

Ceilings under roofs

A roof or attic leak leaves stains and growth on the drywall below.

Behind appliances

Slow leaks from washers and refrigerator lines leave a hidden damp area.

What removal involves

1

Find and fix the moisture

A plumber, roofer or drainage contractor stops the water source.

2

Contain the work area

Plastic sheeting and negative air limit the spread of disturbed material.

3

Remove affected porous materials

Drywall, insulation and carpet with growth are removed and bagged.

4

Clean the framing

HEPA vacuums and wiping clean surfaces of structural materials.

5

Dry and verify

Readings confirm the area is dry before anything is closed up.

Should I test?

The state health department does not recommend routine testing for mold, because there are no standards to compare results against. It is more useful to find the moisture and remove the affected material than to measure spores. A written scope from a contractor, with what will be removed and how the area will be contained, tells you more than a lab number.

DIY cleaning and its limits

Small spots on a hard, non-porous surface can often be cleaned with detergent and water while you wear gloves and a mask rated for the job. Larger areas, anything inside walls or on porous material, and any growth that returns are better handled with containment. Scrubbing a large area dry can send material through the house. See our mold remediation page for the professional approach.

Health questions

We do not make claims about the health effects of mold. If you or a family member has concerns, speak with a doctor. Our role is practical: find the moisture, remove affected materials, contain the work and dry the building.

Protecting the rest of the house while you decide

If you find growth and are not ready to hire anyone, a few steps keep things from getting worse. Fix or stop the water source if you can. Keep the area dry by using a dehumidifier and moving air. Do not sand, scrub dry or use a leaf blower or household fan in the room, since these spread material.

Close the door to the room, and turn off a central system that returns air from that space. Avoid storing food or bedding in the area. Photograph the growth and the surrounding stains so you can compare later. These steps buy time and limit spread, but they do not replace removal of affected porous materials.

What state and local sources say

What the S520 standard says about the work

The published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard guides mold testing and remediation, from finding the moisture source to checking the result. Ask any contractor whether its crews work to S520 before you sign. Color alone does not tell you what a growth is, which is why moisture and inspection come first.
