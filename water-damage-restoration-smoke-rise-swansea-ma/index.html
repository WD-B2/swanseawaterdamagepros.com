A septic neighborhood

Smoke Rise is a residential neighborhood in Swansea, with streets such as Smoke Rise Circle. Swansea's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report lists Smoke Rise among its vulnerable neighborhoods, citing about 500 homes with septic problems on poor soils. That is a town planning figure. It does not say that your system has a problem.

Swansea has no public sewer system and relies on private septic systems. A 2025 state water resources decision says those systems have been failing and causing water quality concerns, and notes an agreement with Somerset on wastewater services that has not yet produced a connection. We do not say when or whether Smoke Rise will be connected.

When wastewater comes back into the house

A backup of sewage into a tub, floor drain or basement is a contamination event. Keep people and pets out, stop using water, and call a septic professional about the system. The septic company fixes the source. We deal with what the water touched: removal, cleaning and drying.

Household fans and ordinary vacuums are not suitable for sewage. Porous materials such as carpet, pad and low drywall that were wet with sewage are often removed rather than dried. Read sewage backup cleanup for what that involves.

What cleanup after a backup involves

The crew first confirms that the source has been stopped and that the area is safe to enter. Standing liquid is removed with equipment, and materials that cannot be cleaned, such as soaked carpet pad and low drywall, are taken out and bagged. Hard surfaces are then cleaned and disinfected.

Drying follows, with moisture readings repeated until the framing and floor are back to normal. Your septic professional decides what is needed for the tank and drain field, and your building department can tell you about permits for repairs.

Safe steps for a backup

  • Stop using toilets, sinks and washers so nothing more enters the system
  • Keep children, pets and everyone else out of the affected area
  • Do not touch the water with bare hands and do not stand in it near electrical items
  • Photograph the damage from a safe spot
  • Call a septic professional for the source and call us for the cleanup

Insurance for a backup

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says sewer or drain backup is generally excluded from a standard homeowners policy unless you add an endorsement. Ask your agent whether you have one. Photos, moisture readings and a record of the work can help document a loss, but your carrier decides coverage.
