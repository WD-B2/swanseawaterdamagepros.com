Treat a wet ceiling as a hazard

A ceiling holding water is heavy. Drywall can bulge, crack or collapse, and plaster can let go in sheets. If you see a bulge, drips or a growing brown stain, move people and furniture out from under it. A small hole poked in the lowest part of a bulge lets the water drain into a bucket, but only if you can do it safely. If there is any doubt, leave it and call.

A dripping or bulging ceiling is a safety issue

Water collects above a ceiling until the drywall gives way. A bulge, a drip or a stain that grows is a sign to act now. Keep clear of the area, move belongings and call. The line is answered after hours, so a ceiling that starts dripping at night can still be reported.

Find the source before you fix the ceiling

Ceiling water comes from above, and the source decides the fix. A plumbing leak from a bathroom or water line is stopped by a plumber. A roof leak is a roofing problem. Condensation on an air handler in the attic is an HVAC issue, which we discuss on our AC leak page. We deal with the water that has already arrived and the materials it soaked.

Roof leaks

Wind-driven rain, lifted shingles and failed flashing let water in at the roof deck. Staining often shows up several feet from the actual opening.

Plumbing above

A tub overflow, toilet supply or bathroom drain leaks onto the ceiling below.

Ice dams

Snow melting and refreezing at the eaves backs water under shingles. Late winter stains are a classic sign.

Attic condensation

Warm moist air rising into a cold attic condenses on the roof deck and drips down.

What gets inspected

Insulation above a wet ceiling holds water long after the surface dries. We use moisture meters and a thermal camera to trace the wet area and check the attic or the floor above when access allows. Plaster over lath, which is common in older Swansea houses, behaves differently from modern drywall, and the repair approach changes with it.

The cleanup sequence

1

Protect the room

Furniture and belongings are moved or covered, and drips are caught.

2

Open the wet section

Sagging or saturated ceiling material is removed in a controlled way so the cavity can be reached.

3

Remove wet insulation

Insulation that cannot dry in place is bagged and taken out.

4

Dry the structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the joists, decking and surrounding walls.

5

Hand off for repair

Once readings are normal, patching, texture and paint can proceed.

Stains are not always old damage

A brown ring on a ceiling may be a past leak that has dried, or it may be a slow leak that is still active. A moisture meter answers that question better than touch or sight. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health observes that moisture is the underlying cause of mold, so a stain that stays damp deserves attention even if the leak seems minor.

Repairs and permits

Swansea's building department lists re-roofing as work that requires a permit, along with plumbing repairs. If the fix involves the roof or the plumbing, ask the contractor who will pull the permit and what inspections apply. Our job is the water and the drying, and we coordinate our timing with the trades who fix the source.

If the water also reached the wall below, our wall and drywall page describes that repair. For leaks that started with a pipe, see burst pipe water damage.

The standard we measure against

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water damage restoration. It emphasizes finding the full extent of moisture instead of judging by the visible stain, which is why we use meters and a thermal camera on ceilings.
