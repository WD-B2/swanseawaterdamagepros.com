Storms Swansea remembers

The town's hazard report records the hurricanes of 1938 and 1954, when water came over the sea wall and homes shifted off foundations. March 2010 brought days of rain, flooded roads and closed bridges. Flood-prone neighborhoods include the southern end of town, the Grove, Touisset, Bushee Road and Cove Street. The same report notes that the boat ramp is underwater about five times a year at normal high tides, a small sign of how close the water already sits.

When the storm is still going

Wind-driven rain and roof openings let water in while the storm is still underway. If it is safe, move belongings away from the wet area and call. The line is answered during storms, at night and on weekends, even when the power is out.

How storms get water inside

Roof and flashing

Wind lifts shingles and rain gets under flashing at chimneys and dormers.

Windows and doors

Wind-driven rain forces water past old seals and into walls.

Basements and foundations

Saturated soil pushes water through cracks and the joint at the floor.

Sump pump failure

During an outage, a sump with no battery backup stops and the basement fills.

Power outages make it worse

Storms bring outages. Without power, sump pumps stop, dehumidifiers go off and heat fails. Water that would have been controlled spreads. In Rehoboth, a March 2018 storm left about 85 percent of the town without power and closed 32 roads. A house that sits wet in a storm outage may need more than water removal. Ask us before turning equipment on if you are unsure about electrical safety.

Storm damage and insurance

Wind and rain damage through a damaged roof or window is generally treated differently from flood and ground water. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard policies exclude flood, and that basement seepage is excluded. Keep temporary repair receipts and take pictures. We do not decide coverage.

Repairs and permits

Roofing, siding and window replacement require permits under Swansea's building department. We dry the structure and coordinate timing with the contractors who repair the envelope. Read our pages on flood damage cleanup, ceiling water damage and basement water damage for the common storm losses.

Heavy rain can reach homes well inland of the bay, and Swansea's hazard report lists the Touisset and Bushee Road area as vulnerable. See our page for Touisset.

Before and after a storm

A few steps limit the damage. Before a forecast storm, clear gutters, test the sump pump and move valuables off the basement floor. Know where your main water valve and electrical panel are. After a storm, walk the property from the outside first and look for lifted shingles, broken windows and standing water near the foundation. Take photos with dates.

If a tree or limb has fallen on the house, stay clear until it has been inspected. Call us once the area is safe, and we will advise what to do with any water inside.

Sump pumps with battery backups or water-powered backups keep running when the power goes out, and a generator can power the pump if used safely outdoors. These are simple precautions that can prevent the worst storm losses in low-lying basements.
