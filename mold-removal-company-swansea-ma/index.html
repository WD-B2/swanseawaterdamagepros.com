How to Choose a Mold Removal Company in Swansea, MA

A good contractor writes down what will be removed, how the area will be contained and how drying will be confirmed. Here are the questions that separate a real scope from a sales pitch.

Start with the scope, not the price

Two quotes for mold work can differ by thousands because they cover different work. One company contains the area and removes the drywall. Another sprays and paints. Ask each to write down what will be removed, what will be cleaned, how the area will be contained and how drying will be confirmed. If a contractor will not put that in writing, move on.

Questions to ask

  • What materials will you remove, and where does the scope stop?
  • How will you contain the area and protect the rest of the house?
  • What will you do about the moisture source?
  • How will you confirm the area is dry before closing up?
  • What records will I receive, and in what form?
  • What is not included, such as rebuilding, painting or plumbing?

Licensing varies by state

Rules about mold work differ from one state to another. Ask whether your state licenses mold work and what that covers. Ask any company what standards they follow and request references to documentation you can review. Be cautious about anyone who claims credentials they cannot show, and avoid claims that sound too good to be true.

Be careful with testing sales pitches

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health does not recommend routine mold testing because there are no standards to compare results against. A company that insists on expensive testing before any work may be selling the test. A useful inspection looks at moisture, materials and the extent of growth, and results in a written plan.

Red flags

  • Quotes that do not mention moisture or the source of the water
  • Promises to eliminate mold forever
  • No containment, or containment that amounts to a sheet taped over a door
  • Pressure to sign the same day
  • A refusal to explain what will be removed

Who does what

Mold remediation, plumbing, roofing and rebuilding are separate trades. Some companies do all of them and some do only one. Ask who handles the water source, who rebuilds and who pulls permits. In Swansea, plumbing repair and re-roofing require permits from the building department, so someone needs to be responsible for them.

What our process looks like

We find the moisture, contain the area, remove affected porous materials, clean framing and dry the structure. Readings confirm it before anyone closes the wall. We do not make health claims about mold and do not do the plumbing or rebuilding. Details are on our mold remediation page, and drying is on our structural drying page.

How to compare two written scopes

Lay the two scopes next to each other. Does each one list the rooms and the square footage involved? Does each name the materials to be removed, such as drywall to a specific height, insulation or baseboards? Does each describe containment, such as plastic barriers, negative air and a decontamination area? Does each say how drying will be measured and what readings count as dry?

Does each say what is excluded? A scope that is vague on these points is vague on price too, and the cheaper one may be doing less. Ask both contractors to walk you through the lines that differ, and choose the one that explains the work clearly and puts it in writing.

After the work is done

Ask for the final documentation before you make the last payment. It should include photos from before and after, the moisture readings that show the area is dry, and a short description of what was removed and cleaned. Keep these with your home records. If you sell the house later, or if moisture returns, they show what was done and when. If anything on the invoice differs from the scope, ask about it before you pay.

Local and state checkpoints

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health says moisture is the underlying cause of mold and that testing is not routinely recommended. Use that as a filter when a company leads with testing.

Swansea's building department lists plumbing, mechanical, electrical, re-roofing and replacement window work among the jobs that require permits, and it asks homeowners to check with the department about required inspections. If remediation leads to repair work in any of those areas, ask the contractor who will pull the permit.

We also serve Rehoboth and Assonet, and the questions above apply no matter which town you live in.

Ask about the S520 standard

Testing and remediation follow the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, so a fair question for any company is whether its crews work to it. A clear answer, with a description of containment, air filtering and verification, tells you more than a promise on a flyer.
