A peninsula between two rivers

Mass Audubon describes Gardner's Neck as a small residential peninsula. The Lee and Cole rivers empty into Mount Hope Bay on either side of it, and a sandy spit runs west from the Neck into the Cole River. Most of the shoreline is privately owned and developed.

The border between Swansea and Somerset runs down the Lee River, so Somerset is across the water. Homeowners on the Neck who are not sure which town they are in can call and give us the address. See also our Somerset page.

How the Neck came to be a residential area

A state historical survey says the South Swansea railway station opened the Neck to Fall River merchants and mill owners as summer residents, and that it was largely suburbanized by the end of the late industrial period. It names The Colony on Gardner's Neck Road for its summer houses, and notes more building in the early 1920s along the streetcar lines.

The result is a mix of house ages and styles on one road system. We do not say that any era of house is more likely to leak. What the mix means in practice is that we ask what is behind the wall before we decide how to dry it, and we do not assume what the plumbing is made of.

Water risks on a waterfront peninsula

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, and Swansea's town report does not name Gardners Neck. It does list coastal flooding, damaged sea walls and eroding beaches as concerns at the southern end of town. If your home is near the shore, look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your agent about flood coverage.

Safe steps before the crew plans the job

  • Keep everyone out of water that may touch outlets, a heater or the panel
  • Close the main water valve if you can reach it without wading
  • Do not run household fans or an ordinary vacuum in storm or river water
  • Photograph the damage and the waterline from a safe spot
  • Call us with the street, the water depth and the source

Insurance and the record of the work

Standard homeowners policies commonly exclude flood, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, while a burst pipe is treated differently. That is why the cause of the water matters. We keep photos, moisture readings and notes on what was dried or removed so you have a record, and your carrier makes the coverage decision.
