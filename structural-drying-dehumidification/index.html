Does your house still feel damp after a leak?

The leak is fixed and the puddle is gone, but something is off. The house smells musty. A hardwood floor has started to cup. Baseboards feel cool and clammy, paint is bubbling or wallpaper is lifting. These are the signs of moisture left behind in materials, and they are the reason drying is a separate job from water removal.

  • A musty smell that returns after cleaning
  • Floors that crown, cup or buckle
  • Baseboard paint that bubbles or peels
  • Windows that fog on the inside in rooms near the leak
  • Walls that feel cold or damp to the touch

What structural drying is

Structural drying removes moisture from the building materials themselves. After standing water is out, wood framing, subfloor, concrete and drywall still hold water. The job is to move that moisture into the air and then out of the building. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces. Dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air so it cannot settle back in. The two together are what finish a job.

Measuring comes before drying

We start with moisture meters and a thermal camera. Meters give numbers for specific materials, and thermal imaging shows cold, wet areas behind a surface. We take readings on unaffected, dry rooms too, so we know what dry looks like in your house. That comparison is what lets us say the building is dry instead of guessing.

1

Map the wet area

Readings are taken across walls, floors and ceilings and recorded.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed where the readings call for them.

Readings are repeated and equipment is adjusted as materials dry.

Equipment comes out only when readings match the dry reference.

Why humidity matters in a south coast home

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health notes that mold growth can occur when relative humidity stays above roughly 70 percent for a few days. It also says absorbent materials not dried within 24 to 48 hours often need replacement. Near Mount Hope Bay and the river corridors, summer air is already damp, which gives a wet building less room for error. Controlled dehumidification matters more than open windows.

Common jobs that end in drying

Most losses finish with drying. Water extraction takes out the bulk water. A burst pipe, a water heater leak or a wet carpet each leave moisture behind that equipment has to pull out. If drying was started too late or stopped too soon, mold remediation may follow.

Equipment you will see in the house

Air movers look like small fans and push air along surfaces. LGR dehumidifiers, a type designed to remove moisture efficiently at low humidity, pull water out of the air and drain it away. Moisture meters read wood and drywall, and a thermal camera shows cold, wet spots.

In some jobs, drying mats pull moisture through a hard floor, and a HEPA air scrubber cleans the air when material has been opened. We explain what each piece does and where it will sit.

Drying equipment is loud and uses electricity. It runs around the clock for several days in a typical job, and we explain how long we expect it to run based on the readings. We take daily measurements so you can see progress. When readings are normal, we remove the equipment and give you the record.

The standard behind structural drying

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published industry reference for water damage restoration, and it is built around measured drying toward a documented goal. The moisture mapping and daily readings on this page follow that idea.

What to do and what not to do during drying

  • Do not turn off drying equipment to save power or reduce noise
  • Keep doors and windows closed in the drying area
  • Do not move or unplug the equipment
  • Tell us if a breaker trips or a unit stops running
  • Keep children and pets away from cords and equipment
