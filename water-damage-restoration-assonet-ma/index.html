The Assonet River floodplain

The Freetown hazard planning documents place the Assonet River floodplain through the center of the village. In 2010, ten days of rain washed out bridges in town. Flood-prone spots include the Assonet Bay Shores causeway, Beach Bluff Road, Dr. Brailey Road and the Chippewa Road culvert on Fall Brook. The Elm Street fire station sits in a flood zone, which tells you how close the water runs to the center of town.

When water is spreading in Assonet

Find the shut-off

Older village houses can have valves in odd spots. Close the main valve where the line enters the house and the one at the fixture.

Mind the old wiring

Do not touch outlets or switches near wet plaster or a flooded basement. Leave the breaker alone if you have to stand in water to reach it.

Look behind the surface

Water can run behind plaster and trim. Note any stains, soft spots or bulges and photograph them.

Call with the story

Tell us the age of the house, the source of the water and how long it has been wet.

Wells, septic and what that means for leaks

The Freetown Water Commission supplies Assonet with water that comes from Fall River, but most residents use private wells and septic systems. Septic systems close to the river can fail when ground water is high, and wastewater can back up into a basement. That is a sewage backup situation, and we treat it as contaminated.

Working in an older village home

Older houses behave differently when wet. Plaster over lath can dry in place where drywall would have to come out, but it can also crack and let go as it dries. Original wood floors may be worth saving, and a careful drying plan is how that happens. Stone and brick foundations let moisture through more readily than poured concrete.
