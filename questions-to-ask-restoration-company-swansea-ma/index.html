Questions to Ask a Swansea, MA Water Damage Company

The right questions take five minutes and reveal how a company works. Ask them before anyone sets a single fan in your house.

Why the first call matters more than the quote

When water is in the house, people hire the first company that answers. That is understandable, and it is also where problems start. A short phone conversation tells you a lot. Does the person ask where the water came from, how long it has been there and whether power is affected? Do they tell you what to shut off and what to photograph? A company that goes straight to a price without those questions is selling, not assessing.

Questions about the work

  • Who is the person in charge on site, and who will I talk to during the job?
  • How will you measure moisture, and will I see the readings?
  • What equipment will you set, and how long do you expect it to run?
  • What will you remove, and how will you tell me when something cannot be dried?
  • How will you decide the building is dry?

A real answer to the last question mentions readings and a comparison with dry reference materials. A weak answer says it will be dry in three days. Drying time depends on the materials and the amount of water, and honest contractors say so. Our structural drying page describes what a measured process looks like.

Questions about money

Ask how the job is priced and what drives the total. Water damage costs depend on the amount of water, the materials affected, the equipment days and the amount of material removed. A fair contractor can explain those factors before work starts. Ask what is included, what is extra and what happens if the job grows. Ask for a written scope before work begins, and for an itemized invoice at the end.

Be wary of a flat number given over the phone with no look at the damage, and of any request for a large payment up front. Ask whether the company works directly with your insurance carrier, and what documentation they provide for your claim. The company documents the loss. Your carrier decides coverage.

Questions about paperwork and permits

  • Will you provide photos, readings and a description of the work?
  • Do you carry insurance, and can you show it to me?
  • Who handles permits if the repair requires them?
  • What do you subcontract, and to whom?
  • Do you handle the rebuild, or only the water and the drying?

Swansea requires permits for plumbing repair and several other trades. If a restoration company also repairs pipes or the roof, ask who pulls the permit. If it only dries, ask who the plumber or roofer will be and how the two jobs will be scheduled together.

Questions about contamination and safety

If the water came from a drain, a flood or a backup, ask how it will be handled differently. Contaminated water requires different protective equipment, different disposal and different cleaning than a clean supply line. A company that treats a sewage loss the same as a washing machine overflow is cutting corners. Our pages on sewage backup cleanup and flood damage cleanup explain the difference.

Signs of a company worth hiring

They explain the plan

You understand what will be done and why before it begins.

They measure and record

You see readings and photos, not just invoices.

They say what they do not do

Plumbing, roofing and rebuilding are named as separate trades.

They do not pressure you

You can take a few minutes to think without a deadline on the quote.

Local and state facts to keep in mind

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises reporting a loss promptly and reading your policy carefully, and it notes that standard policies exclude flood and basement seepage. Ask any contractor to explain what is documented, because that is what your carrier will want.

Swansea's building department states that a permit is required for any installation or repair of plumbing, and for items such as re-roofing, electrical and mechanical work. It also says to check with the department for required inspections. A good contractor already knows this.

What to do before you hire

  • Ask two or three companies for a written scope
  • Do not sign on the spot under pressure
  • Do not pay a large deposit before you see the scope
  • Ask what they do about contaminated water
  • Photograph the damage so you can compare what each company says
