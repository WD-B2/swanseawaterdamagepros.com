Wells, pumps and what happens when they fail

The Dighton Water District serves about 1,800 connections from wells on Walker and Cedar Street, supplemented by Taunton water. About 1,300 households rely on private wells. Private well systems depend on a pump, a pressure tank and lines that run across the property. When one fails, a leak can run for a long time before anyone notices. Frozen pressure lines and well house flooding are common winter problems.

Septic and wastewater

According to the town's planning documents, only about 600 of 3,200 households, around 19 percent, are sewered, mostly in North Dighton and tied to Taunton. The remaining 81 percent are on septic. High ground water saturates leach fields, and a failing septic system can back wastewater into a basement. That is a contaminated loss and we treat it as one. Read more on sewage backup cleanup.

Where Dighton floods

The town hazard report lists Route 138, Lincoln Avenue over the Three Mile River, Lower Main Street, Cobbs Lane, Water Street, Dighton Rock Park and Rebecca and Susan Roads among the flood-prone areas. It also notes the high hazard Muddy Cove Brook Dam. The 1938 hurricane threw a South Dighton cottage about 100 yards. Storm damage in these areas can include basement flooding and roof loss, covered on our storm water damage cleanup page.

When water is spreading in Dighton

Stop the pump

If you are on a well, switch off the pump breaker before closing valves. A running pump can keep feeding a broken line.

Close the house valve

Shut the main valve where the line enters the house. Check the pressure tank area for leaks as well.

Protect the electrics

Do not step into standing water near the panel, the well controls or the furnace. Call the utility if the power is unsafe.

Call with the details

Tell us the source, the depth and whether the property is on a well, so we can plan the equipment.

Dispatch for Dighton runs around the clock, so a well or septic problem that starts after dark does not wait for the morning.
