Signs sewage may be backing up

  • Toilets that will not flush or bubble when another fixture drains
  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once
  • Dark water coming up through a basement floor drain or tub
  • A sewage smell inside the house
  • Wet ground or standing water over a septic tank or leach field

Any one of these can be a simple clog. Several together, or water of unknown color at floor level, is a reason to stop using water and call. Do not flush, run the dishwasher or run the washer.

Why this water is treated as contaminated

Sewage carries bacteria, viruses and parasites. Cleanup is not a mop and bucket job. Porous materials such as carpet, padding, drywall and insulation that touched sewage usually cannot be sanitized and are removed. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated. The crew uses protective gear, containment where needed and disposal that matches the material.

Sewage in the house is a health hazard

Raw sewage carries bacteria, so keep children, pets and everyone else out of the affected area, do not run more water and avoid touching it. Call right away at any hour. The line is staffed at night and on weekends because backups do not wait.

Swansea has two very different systems

Many homes in this area rely on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer, and Dighton is mostly on septic. The town's hazard mitigation report notes that roughly 500 homes in the Smoke Rise area have septic problems, and high ground water can saturate a leach field and slow or stop it. When that happens, wastewater has nowhere to go and may back into the house.

Neighboring Somerset is on public sewer, but its report shows most of its pump stations lie within projected flood boundaries, so backups can happen there during storms.

Who fixes the source depends on the system. A septic pumper or contractor handles the tank and field. A municipal sewer backup may involve the public works department. Either way, call a plumber to clear a line and call us for the cleanup.

What the cleanup includes

1

Stop the source

Water use stops, and the backup is identified as a drain line, a septic issue or a municipal problem.

2

Remove the liquid

Contaminated water is pumped and vacuumed out and handled as contaminated waste.

3

Remove porous materials

Carpet, padding, insulation and drywall that touched sewage are cut out and bagged.

4

Clean and treat

Remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products suited to the surface.

5

Dry and verify

Equipment dries the structure, and readings confirm it before rebuild.

What insurance usually says about sewer backup

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance notes that sewer backup and sump pump failure are excluded from standard homeowners policies by default, though endorsements are available. Check whether yours includes one. Report the loss promptly and ask what documentation your carrier wants. We provide photos and moisture and cleaning records and do not decide coverage.

Protect yourself and others

  • Keep children and pets out of the area
  • Wear gloves and boots if you must enter, and wash thoroughly afterward
  • Do not use a household vacuum on sewage
  • Do not run the furnace if ducts or the unit were in the water
  • Throw away food and items that touched the water

In Swansea, the town's own hazard report cites septic problems in the Smoke Rise neighborhood, and the same advice applies there: keep people out, call a septic professional for the source and call us for the cleanup.

Many policies require a specific sewer or drain backup endorsement, so ask your carrier whether you have it.

After the cleanup

Once the area is dry, replace what was removed and look into what caused the backup. A plumber can camera-inspect a drain line to find a blockage, root intrusion or a collapsed section. A septic contractor can check the tank, the baffles and the leach field. If your area is on municipal sewer, report the backup to the public works department so they can check the main.

A backwater valve can reduce the chance of a repeat, and the sewer backup endorsement is worth asking your agent about at the next renewal.
