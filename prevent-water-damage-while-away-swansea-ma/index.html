Prevent Water Damage While Away From Your Swansea, MA Home

Most major water losses happen while nobody is home. A short checklist before you leave reduces the risk and shortens the response if something does fail.

Why empty houses get hit hardest

A pipe that bursts while you are home gets noticed in minutes. The same pipe in an empty house can run for days. Vacations, winters in Florida and seasonal use of a cottage near the bay leave homes unobserved. The goal of a pre-trip routine is to shut off what can fail and arrange eyes on the place.

Before you leave

  • Shut off the main water valve, or at least the valves to the washer and dishwasher
  • Set the heat to a steady temperature, not off, in winter
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls in cold weather
  • Check hoses behind the washer and refrigerator and replace any that bulge
  • Turn the water heater to vacation mode or off if you will be away a long time
  • Clear gutters and extend downspouts if a storm is likely
  • Test the sump pump and, if you have one, the battery backup

Arrange a check-in

Ask a neighbor or friend to walk through every few days, especially after a storm or cold snap. Give them the main shutoff location, your phone number and the number of a plumber you trust. Smart leak sensors and monitored thermostats can send an alert to your phone, which is worth considering for a seasonal property.

What to tell your insurer

Many policies have conditions for vacant or unoccupied homes, such as keeping the heat on or having someone check the house at set intervals. Ask your agent what yours says. Following the conditions can make the difference in a freeze claim. Our frozen pipe water damage page explains what happens after a freeze.

Seasonal homes on the bay

Many shoreline cottages in Swansea and nearby towns were built for summer. Some have exposed plumbing, minimal insulation and crawl spaces that vent to the wind. If you close one for the winter, drain the system or ask a plumber how to winterize it. Leaving the water on and the heat low is a common cause of January losses.

If something does fail

1

Close the main valve

If a neighbor is on site, they should close it first.

2

Call a plumber and a restoration crew

3

Document everything

Photos, the shutoff and any damaged items are useful for your claim.

4

Notify your carrier

Report promptly and ask what is required for an unoccupied home.

A simple pre-trip routine you can repeat

Keep the checklist on the fridge or in your phone and run through it every time you leave for more than a couple of days. Walk the house and look at the washer hoses, the water heater, the dishwasher and under each sink. Note any damp spot and fix it first. Photograph the main shutoff valve and share the photo with your neighbor.

Take a quick video of each room before you leave so you have a record of its condition. Turn off the ice maker and drain the line if you will be gone a long time. None of this takes more than half an hour, and it turns a possible disaster into a short phone call.

If you rent the place out

Short-term rentals and guest houses add risk, because guests do not know where the shutoff valve is and may not report a small leak. Put the location of the main valve and a phone number for emergencies on a card in the kitchen. Ask the cleaning crew to check under sinks and around the water heater between stays, and to report anything damp immediately.

A written checklist for turnovers is cheap protection, and it gives you a record if a claim ever comes up.

Local conditions worth knowing

Swansea's hazard mitigation report lists the southern end of town, the Grove, Touisset and Cove Street as flood-prone, and records March 2010 floods that closed schools and cut off emergency access, with residents losing furnaces and water heaters. Homes in those areas should be checked after every major storm, not just in winter.

The Swansea Building Department requires a permit for plumbing installation or repair, so any winterizing or upgrade that changes the plumbing should be done through a plumber who knows the process.

If you own property in Somerset or Dighton, the same checklist applies. See our water heater leak page for one of the most common causes.

Where the industry reference fits

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published industry reference for water damage restoration, and it stresses that damage grows with time. An unattended house is the clearest example, which is the reason this checklist exists.
