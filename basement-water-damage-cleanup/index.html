Basements are the lowest point on the property

Water finds the lowest point. In a house on the south coast, that is the basement or the crawl space. Swansea sits beside Mount Hope Bay and between river corridors, and the town hazard mitigation report points to areas where the water table and storm surge push against foundations. When the ground is saturated, water enters through the slab joint, wall cracks and window wells.

Seepage

Water seeps through walls and floor after heavy rain or rising ground water.

Sump failure

A sump pump that fails or loses power lets the basement fill.

Plumbing

A burst pipe or failed water heater floods from the inside.

Backup

Floor drains and sewer lines can back up during storms.

When water is rising in the basement

Water in a basement can reach outlets, the furnace and stored belongings quickly. Stay off the stairs if water is deep and do not touch electrical equipment. Call at any hour and tell us how deep it is and where it is coming from.

Safety first

Water in a basement and electrical panels or outlets do not mix. If water is touching outlets, a furnace or the panel, stay out. Call your electric utility or an electrician. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas company. We ask about the electrical situation on the first call before anyone steps into the water.

What insurance says about basements

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states that standard homeowners policies exclude basement seepage and that no endorsement is available for it. Sewer backup and sump pump failure are excluded by default, but endorsements exist. Sudden discharge from a burst pipe is often covered. Which one applies to you depends on the source, so describe what happened carefully. We do not decide coverage.

Finished basements and stone foundations

Finished basements add drywall, carpet, insulation and furniture that soak up water. Older stone and block foundations let moisture through more readily than poured concrete. Both make drying a longer job. Our pages on carpet water damage and wall and drywall repair describe the finished-space side.

Keep it from happening again

  • Test your sump pump and consider a battery backup
  • Clear gutters and extend downspouts away from the foundation
  • Keep valuables on shelves and off the floor
  • Know where your floor drain and cleanout are
  • Check after every major storm

Stored belongings and what to do with them

Basements hold decades of storage. Cardboard boxes wick water upward and collapse, and papers and photographs stuck together are hard to save. Move what you can to a dry, cool place on shelves or plastic bins, and photograph items you discard so you have a record. If irreplaceable papers are wet, lay them flat or place them in a freezer bag until a specialist can help.

The crew on site can tell you which items touched contaminated water and should go.
