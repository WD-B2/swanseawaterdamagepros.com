Water damage help for Somerset homes and businesses

Somerset is about 4.4 miles from our Swansea office, and calls from here are routine. The town sits on a peninsula between the Taunton River to the east and the Lee River to the west, and its southern end is effectively an island of low ground. When rain, wind and a high tide line up, water finds the neighborhoods closest to the shore first. Plumbing losses happen everywhere, and we handle those the same way.

Our process is the same wherever the call comes from. We listen to what happened, tell you what to shut off, remove the water and dry the structure. You can read about each stage on our pages for water extraction, structural drying and flood damage cleanup.

When water is spreading in Somerset

Shut the valve first

Close the main valve or the one at the fixture. If a basement near the river has water at the outlets, stay out and call the utility about the power.

Keep drains clear

In a storm or high tide, floor drains can push water up. Move belongings away from them and hold off on laundry and dishwashers.

Raise it and photograph it

Lift valuables onto shelves or to an upper floor, and photograph the waterline before you move much.

Call with the street and source

Give the address, where the water is coming from and how deep it is so the crew can plan the visit.

Dispatch for Somerset runs around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays, and a storm tide at 2 a.m. gets the same attention as a weekday leak.

What the town's own hazard report says

Somerset's hazard mitigation plan describes a town shaped by water. It records the hurricanes of 1938 and Carol in 1954, then Gloria in 1985 and Bob in 1991, and the March 2010 storm that dropped about 16 inches of rain. The Route 103 and Route 6 causeways sit below the 1 percent base flood elevation, which means a major storm can cut roads that residents rely on.

Waterfront Park is often underwater during moon tides, and the Riverside Avenue culvert at Buffington Brook is one of the places the plan flags.

The plan also identifies Brayton Point, a 365 acre site on the shore, and the high hazard rating of the Somerset Reservoir Dam. For a homeowner those are not daily concerns, but they explain why flood and storm losses are part of life here. Our storm water damage page covers what that cleanup involves.

Sewer backups are a Somerset issue

The whole town of Somerset is on public sewer, which is convenient until it fails. According to the town report, 15 of the 17 pump stations lie within projected flood boundaries. When a station floods or loses power, wastewater can back up into low-lying homes through floor drains and tubs. That is contaminated water.

See our sewage backup cleanup page for the process, and remember that the Massachusetts Division of Insurance says sewer backup is excluded from standard policies unless you add an endorsement.
