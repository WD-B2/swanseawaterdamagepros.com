What to do the moment you see the tank leaking

  • Close the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater
  • Switch off the electric breaker or set a gas unit to pilot or off
  • Keep out of standing water if the heater or outlets sit in it
  • Photograph the tank, the label and the wet area before cleanup
  • Call us for the water and a plumber for the heater

Do not drain the tank into a flooded room. A plumber or the manufacturer's instructions will cover that step once the area is safe.

A leaking tank can empty fast

A tank holds a lot of water, and a failed seam can release it in minutes. Turn off the power or gas to the heater and close the cold water supply to it, then call. Our line takes after-hours calls, because tanks tend to fail whenever they please.

Why tanks fail when they do

A storage water heater is a steel tank lined with glass. Over time the lining cracks, minerals settle to the bottom and the sacrificial anode rod wears out. Then the steel corrodes from the inside until it seeps or ruptures. Most failures show first as a damp spot, rust streaks on the base or a puddle on the floor. A rupture is the dramatic version, usually after a house has been ignoring a slow weep for months.

Swansea homes draw from nine active town wells treated for corrosion control, according to the Swansea Water District's source water assessment, while neighboring Dighton and Rehoboth rely heavily on private wells. Hardness and sediment vary a great deal from one water source to another, and that affects how fast a tank loads up with scale. It is one more reason an old tank near the end of its life can let go with little warning.

Where the water ends up

Water heaters live in basements, utility closets, attics and garages. In a basement, the water spreads across the slab and soaks the bottom of framed walls and any boxed items stored on the floor. In a closet on an upper floor, it drops through the subfloor into the ceiling below, which brings us into ceiling water damage repair. In either case, wet insulation and drywall at the baseboard are the first things to check.

The cleanup

1

Remove standing water

Wet vacuums and pumps clear the floor. Stored boxes and furniture are lifted out and set on blocks.

2

Check the walls

Moisture meters read the lower few feet of every wall around the heater, which is where wicking shows up.

3

Dry the area

Air movers and a dehumidifier dry slab, framing and any flooring that can be saved.

4

Remove what will not dry

Soaked carpet padding, cardboard, insulation and drywall at the baseboard come out if they cannot be dried in place.

Replacement, permits and timing

Replacing a water heater is plumbing work, and Swansea's building department requires a permit for any installation or repair of plumbing. Gas units also bring a gas line and venting inspection into the picture. Ask your plumber who handles the permit. We schedule our drying around the replacement so the new heater does not go in over a wet floor.

Policies often separate a sudden tank failure from long-term leakage, so ask your carrier how it views yours.

If you want to check your own heater

  • Look for rust at the bottom of the tank and around fittings
  • Check the drain pan, if one is installed, for water
  • Note the manufacture date on the label, because older tanks fail more often
  • Listen for popping or rumbling, which can be sediment
  • Make sure the relief valve pipe is not dripping
