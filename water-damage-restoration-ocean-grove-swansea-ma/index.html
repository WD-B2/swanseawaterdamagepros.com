Ocean Grove sits on the Swansea shore of Mount Hope Bay, with homes along the Cole River. The town history page lists The Plains, Long Point and Long Beach as places within it. Whether the water came from a pipe, a storm tide or a backed-up septic system, the first hours matter, so call and tell us what you see.

What the town report says about the Grove

Swansea's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report lists the Grove among the neighborhoods at flood risk. It also describes the Council on Aging building on Ocean Grove Avenue as prone to flooding. That is a town planning finding about the area, not a statement about any single house.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified on this page. To find out where your own address falls, look it up on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask your insurance agent.

Small homes, close to the water

One real estate guide puts the median year built in Ocean Grove near 1940 and the average single-family home at about 1,172 square feet, with cottage bungalows, split-levels and riverside homes that have private docks. A state survey of Swansea notes that resort building began here in the 1890s, with Colonial Revival, Queen Anne and Shingle Style houses.

We do not claim that older or smaller homes suffer more water damage. What a small footprint does mean is that water can reach most of the floor area quickly, which is a reason to start water extraction early.

Bay or river water in the house

Water that comes in from outside is not clean supply water. Treat it as contaminated and read our flood damage cleanup page before you touch anything.

A failed pipe or fitting

A split line in a bungalow wets floors, cabinet bases and walls fast. See burst pipe water damage.

Septic backup

Swansea has no public sewer, so homes here rely on private septic systems. See sewage backup cleanup.

Wet materials after the water is gone

Floors and wall cavities stay wet after the surface looks dry. See structural drying.

If water is coming in from outside

  • Keep people and pets out of water that may touch outlets, the panel or a heater
  • Do not stand in water to reach a breaker. Call the utility if you are unsure about the power
  • Move valuables up and photograph the waterline before you start cleaning
  • Do not use household fans or an ordinary vacuum on bay or river water
  • Call us with the street, the depth and where the water entered

Flood water and your insurance

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises homeowners to read their policy, since standard policies generally exclude flood. Ask your agent whether you carry separate flood coverage, and report a loss promptly. We keep photos, moisture readings and a record of the work, but your carrier decides what is covered.
