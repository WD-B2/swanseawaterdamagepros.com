Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Swansea, MA Homes

A claim goes better when you stop the damage, document it and report it quickly. Follow these steps in order and keep every receipt and photo.

Step 1: Stop the water and make it safe

Close the main water valve or the valve to the failed fixture. Keep out of standing water near outlets, panels and appliances. If the water is deep or you smell gas, leave the building and call the right emergency service. Your first priority is safety, and your second is limiting the damage, because policies generally expect you to protect the property from getting worse.

Step 2: Photograph everything before you move it

Take wide photos of each room, then close-ups of the source, the water line on walls and each damaged item. Record a slow video walking through the affected areas and narrate what you see. Photograph serial numbers and labels of damaged appliances and electronics. These records are what an adjuster uses to understand the loss when they cannot be there on the first day.

Step 3: Read your policy and call your carrier

Find the declarations page and the water damage language in your policy, and look at the deductible. Then call your insurance company or agent promptly. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises homeowners to report a claim quickly and read the policy carefully. Ask for a claim number, the adjuster's name and what documents they need.

Step 4: Make temporary repairs and keep receipts

Emergency drying, board-ups and a tarp over a damaged roof are examples of reasonable temporary protection. Keep every receipt and invoice. Do not make permanent repairs until the adjuster has seen the damage or approved the plan, unless delay would cause more damage. Our burst pipe and ceiling pages describe what emergency work looks like for those losses.

Step 5: List damaged belongings

  • Describe each item, with brand, model and approximate age
  • Note where you bought it and what it cost, if you remember
  • Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe or contaminated
  • Photograph items before you throw them out
  • Keep a running list as you discover more damage

Step 6: Meet the adjuster and share the records

An adjuster inspects the damage, reviews the policy and estimates the cost to repair. Hand over your photos, receipts and the records from the restoration company. Ask what they will cover and what they will not, and ask for it in writing. If you disagree with the estimate, ask how the process for review works under your policy.

Step 7: Dry, then repair

Drying should start before repairs and finish with documented readings. Repairs proceed after readings show the structure is dry. Rebuilding over wet framing is how mold and rot start. See structural drying and dehumidification for what a proper drying record looks like.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting days before calling the carrier
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them
  • Hiring permanent repairs before the adjuster has seen the loss
  • Assuming flood and seepage are covered when they usually are not
  • Failing to keep a log of calls, names and dates

Keeping a claim log

Start a simple log the moment you find the water. Write the date and time, who you spoke to, what they said and what they promised to do next. Record the claim number on every page. Keep paper and email copies of every estimate, receipt and message. A log looks tedious until the day an adjuster's recollection of a call differs from yours.

It takes a few minutes each day and gives you a clear timeline if anything is questioned later. If drying equipment stays in your house for several days, note the day each piece went in and came out, and keep the daily moisture readings the crew leaves with you. Those records show your carrier that the loss was handled promptly and that the work matched what was needed.

Reporting timelines and Swansea realities

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance recommends reporting claims promptly and reading your policy carefully, and it states that standard policies exclude flood and basement seepage. If your loss came from rising water near the bay, a river or a failed culvert, ask your carrier whether you have separate flood coverage.

Swansea's building department also requires permits for plumbing repair, re-roofing and several other trades. If the repair that follows a loss involves any of them, your contractor should handle the permit, and the department asks homeowners to check with them for required inspections.

If you are in Rehoboth or Assonet, the steps are the same. Call us for the water and the records, and your carrier for the claim.
