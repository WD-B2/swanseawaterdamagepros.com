Swansea, our home town

Swansea is a bayside town of about 17,375 people in Bristol County. It borders Mount Hope Bay and is crossed by river corridors that shape where water goes during storms. The town's hazard mitigation report lists the southern end of town, the Grove, Touisset, Bushee Road, Cove Street and the Smoke Rise area among the places that deal with flooding or septic problems. Our office at 207 Swansea Mall Drive is central to all of it.

How coverage works

Distance and road conditions decide whether we can reach a property quickly, and a storm that closes roads changes that. When you call, we ask for your address and tell you what we can do. We would rather tell you the truth about coverage than promise something we cannot deliver.

How we tell you whether you are in range

Coverage is a practical question, not a map line. On the first call we ask for your street address, look at where the crew would be coming from and think about the road conditions that day. A flooded causeway or a closed bridge changes the answer, and we would rather say so on the phone than leave you waiting. If a crew cannot reach you, we will tell you and suggest what to do in the meantime.

Local conditions we plan around

  • Tidal and storm flooding in low neighborhoods near Mount Hope Bay and the rivers
  • High ground water that pushes into basements and crawl spaces after heavy rain
  • Private wells, pumps and pressure tanks in Dighton, Rehoboth and Assonet
  • Septic systems that fail when the ground is saturated
  • Older homes with galvanized and early copper supply lines
  • Seasonal cottages that freeze when the heat is set too low

Each of these shows up in the calls, and each has its own page of advice. Read the page for your town, then the service page for your problem, and call when you need help.

What to tell us when you call from outside Swansea

A few details speed everything up. Give the street address and the nearest cross street, because rural roads and village streets can be hard to find in the dark. Tell us whether the road is open, since flooding and downed trees close roads in every town on this list. Mention gates, long driveways and animals.

If you are on a private well, say so, because a failed pump or pressure tank is a different problem from a failed city supply line.

If you are calling for a parent, a tenant or a seasonal home, tell us who can let the crew in and how to reach them. We would rather sort out access on the phone than discover a locked door when the water is still running.

What we do in each town

Every town gets the same services, from water extraction and structural drying to mold remediation. The causes differ from place to place. Basement water near the bay, well pump failures in rural lots and sewer backups in low neighborhoods all show up in the calls. Read the page for your town to see what is typical where you live.
