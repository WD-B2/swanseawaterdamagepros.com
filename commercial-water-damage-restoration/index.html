A commercial loss is a different problem

Commercial space is larger, shared and tied to business hours. Water can be in a tenant space, a common corridor or the unit above. There may be a landlord, a property manager and an insurance carrier on each side. The first call needs to cover who owns what, whether the building is occupied and whether power, fire protection and customers are in the picture.

We ask for the size of the space, the type of flooring, the source of the water and any shutoff or access limits. That helps us plan equipment and staffing before the crew reaches the building.

When a business is losing time to water

A wet shop, office or storefront stops work and can ruin stock and records. Shut off the water or power where it is safe and call. The line is answered after hours, which matters when a sprinkler or pipe lets go overnight in a closed building.

Common commercial causes

Supply line and fixture failures

Restroom and break room plumbing fails over a weekend and floods a floor before anyone is in.

Roof and wall leaks

Flat roofs, rooftop units and failed flashing let rain into ceilings and insulation.

Sprinkler and fire line discharge

A pipe burst or head failure releases a high volume of water in a short time.

Storm and flood intrusion

Retail along flood-prone corridors takes water through doors, slabs and drains.

What the response covers

  • Safe isolation of the water source and any affected power
  • Extraction from floors, carpet tile and slab
  • Moisture mapping of walls, ceilings and flooring
  • Drying equipment sized to the space and layout
  • Written records for owner, tenant and insurance use

Businesses on lower ground along these corridors face water risk from storms as well as from internal plumbing failures.

Our office is at 207 Swansea Mall Drive, in the middle of that commercial area, so the businesses here are our neighbors.

Keeping a business running

Not every loss closes a business. Sometimes we can contain water to one room, dry a section at a time and keep the rest open. We tell you honestly whether partial operation makes sense. Safety, slip hazards and electrical conditions decide that, not convenience.

Documentation and insurance

Commercial policies and leases have different requirements from home policies. Landlords and tenants may each have their own carrier. We document the source, the affected areas, the readings and the work so the right people can file what they need. We do not decide coverage or liability.

Planning before the next loss

Businesses that survive water losses usually have a plan. Know where the main shutoff and sprinkler control valves are and make sure staff do too. Keep a list of the property manager, landlord, insurance carrier and an after-hours contact. Store inventory and records off the floor, and keep backups offsite. Walk the space after each major storm and look at ceilings, roof drains and floor drains.

None of this takes long, and it shortens the response when something fails at two in the morning.

For residential parallels, see water extraction and structural drying. We also respond in Somerset and Dighton.

Standards for commercial work

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for professional water damage restoration, and it applies to commercial buildings as well as homes. Documented drying goals matter more in a business, where tenants, owners and carriers may all ask for the record.

What to do before help arrives

  • Shut off the water source and, if the sprinkler system is involved, call the fire alarm company
  • Do not enter areas where water reaches outlets or equipment
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet and slippery areas
  • Move inventory and records above the waterline if it is safe
  • Photograph damage and note the time the water was found
